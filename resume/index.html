Guide

How to use it

Paste a plain duty (e.g. 'handled customer emails'), pick a few details, and get several polished bullet variants built on the action + result + metric formula.

Steps

  1. Paste a plain job duty, such as “handled customer emails”.
  2. Choose a verb style and add a number and a result if you have them.
  3. Copy the bullet that fits best and adjust the details.

Duties into bullets

Lead with a verb, name the work, and stop on a result a stranger can picture. Volume, money, time, or a percent all count. If you cannot measure the result, state an honest scope: a team of four, 30 accounts, a class of 25. Three to five bullets for a recent role is enough. Put the line that matches the job you want first. This page rewrites one duty at a time. It does not lay out the page and it does not invent employers.

Before and after

Handled customer emails is a duty. Answered about 40 customer emails a day and cut the typical reply from a day to under four hours is a bullet, if both numbers are true. About 200 tickets a week is fair when you can show the queue. A 40 percent improvement you cannot explain is not. Look at a CRM export or a calendar before you guess. A bullet that needs three clauses is usually two bullets. The word counter will show when a line has passed about 30 words.

What to leave out

Pronouns, slogans, and references available upon request. Do not put a client's confidential metric, a password, or a government ID in a file you will upload to a job site. Keep the resume as text so an applicant-tracking system can read it. The verb styles only change the opening. Edit the rest until the sentence is something you will say out loud in an interview. The rewrite stays in the browser.

Frequently asked questions

What does a strong bullet contain?

A strong bullet starts with an action verb, names the work, and ends with a result a stranger can picture. Handled customer emails is a duty. Answered about 40 customer emails a day and cut the average reply time from a day to under four hours is a bullet. The number can be volume, money, time, or a percent. If you do not have a measured result, use an honest scope: a team of four, a territory of 30 accounts, a class of 25. Do not invent a percentage. Interviewers ask, and a fabricated metric is worse than a plain true sentence.

How many bullets, and in what order?

Three to five bullets for a recent role is typical. Older roles can have fewer. Put the result that best matches the job you want first, not the task you did most often. A one-page resume for early career and a two-page resume for a long career are both normal. This tool rewrites one duty at a time so you can paste the best line back into your document. It does not design the page, and it does not invent employers.

How do I use numbers I am not sure of?

Use ranges you can defend: about 200 tickets a week, roughly $40,000 in renewals, a queue that fell by around a third. If you cannot defend it, leave the number out. Look at old reports, a CRM export, or a calendar before you guess. The word counter will tell you if a bullet has grown past two lines. A bullet that needs a semicolon and three clauses is usually two bullets.

What should stay out of the bullet?

Pronouns, soft skills with no evidence, and the company's marketing slogan. References available upon request is wasted space. Graphics and photos are a liability in many US hiring pipelines and can confuse applicant-tracking systems that read text. Keep the file as text. Do not put a password, a social security number, or a client's confidential metric in a bullet you will upload to a job site.

How do the verb styles differ?

The styles change the opening verb so you can hear more than one tone. Pick the one that sounds like the work, then edit the rest of the sentence so the verb agrees with the facts. A louder verb does not make a weak result strong. If none of the lines are true, rewrite the duty more specifically and run it again. The tool is a prompt for clarity, not a biography.

Is my work history uploaded?

No. The duty you paste is rewritten in the browser. There is no account and no profile. PDF and Excel exports are local. Clear the box when you are done on a shared computer. The text is only as private as the device and the file you download.

Private by design: everything runs in your browser. Your files and text never leave your device.
