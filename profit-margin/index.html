Guide

How to use it

Use any of the three calculators. Results update as you type: margin and markup from cost and price, the price that hits a target margin or markup, and break-even units with an optional profit goal.

Steps

  1. Enter a cost and a selling price to see profit, margin and markup.
  2. Enter a cost and a target margin (or markup) to get the selling price you need.
  3. Enter fixed costs, price and variable cost per unit to find your break-even point.

Margin, markup, price, and break-even

Margin is profit divided by price. Markup is profit divided by cost. Price for a target margin is cost divided by 1 minus the margin. Price for a target markup is cost times 1 plus the markup. Break-even units are fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost, rounded up. A simple return on the unit cost is profit divided by cost, which is the markup, not the margin.

Worked example

Cost $40 and price $65: profit $25, margin 38.46 percent, markup 62.5 percent. A 40 percent margin on that cost needs 40 divided by 0.60, or $66.67. A 40 percent markup needs only $56.00 and yields a 28.6 percent margin. Fixed costs of $5,000, price $25, variable cost $10: contribution is $15, so break-even is 334 units and $8,350 of revenue. A further $3,000 profit goal needs 534 units.

Where the margin goes

Discounts and card fees are often missing from the unit cost people type. A 15 percent discount and a 3 percent fee can turn a 38 percent calculator margin into something near 20 percent in the accounts. Put inbound freight in cost if you cannot sell without it. Gross margin in the ledger is net sales minus cost of goods sold, divided by net sales. The price ladder shows common target margins on the cost you typed. The business calculators page is the same margin math without the ladder. The freelance rate calculator is the service-business version, where the cost is your year rather than a unit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between margin and markup on a $40 cost and a $65 price?

Profit is $25. Margin is profit divided by the selling price: 25 divided by 65 = 38.46 percent. Markup is profit divided by cost: 25 divided by 40 = 62.5 percent. A 50 percent markup on a $40 cost is a $60 price and only a 33.33 percent margin, because 20 divided by 60 is one third. Quoting markup when a buyer asked for margin is a common way to underprice. This page shows both from the same two numbers so you can answer either question without converting in your head.

How do I set a price from a target margin, and what is a simple ROI check?

Price equals cost divided by 1 minus the target margin. A 40 percent margin on a $40 cost is 40 divided by 0.60 = $66.67. The same cost at a 40 percent markup is 40 times 1.40 = $56.00, which is only a 28.6 percent margin. A simple return on the inventory dollar, sometimes called markup ROI, is profit divided by cost, which is the markup itself: $26.67 profit on $66.67 is a 66.7 percent return on the $40 cost, not a 40 percent return. If you also spend $10 of shipping and fees that are not in cost, put them in cost before you divide or the margin will not survive the order.

How many units cover $5,000 of fixed costs?

Break-even units equal fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit, rounded up to a whole unit. At a $25 price and a $10 variable cost, contribution is $15. $5,000 divided by 15 is 333.33, so you need 334 units. Revenue at that point is 334 times $25 = $8,350. Contribution margin ratio is 15 divided by 25 = 60 percent. To clear an extra $3,000 profit goal, divide 8,000 by 15 and round up to 534 units. If price is not above variable cost, there is no break-even: every sale loses money.

How should a product business use margin in its books?

Gross margin in the accounts is net sales minus cost of goods sold, divided by net sales. Cost of goods includes the unit you typed plus inbound freight and packaging if you cannot sell without them. Payment processing, marketplace fees, and discounts are often outside the unit cost people type here. A 38 percent calculator margin can become a 20 percent accounting margin after a 15 percent discount and a 3 percent card fee. Run the price on the discounted net and put the fee in variable cost. Review the ladder of common margins when a supplier raises cost so you can see the new price before you reprint labels.

When should I use the combined business calculators instead?

Use the four-in-one business calculators page for a fast margin, a single sales-tax line, a loan payment, or a monthly break-even while you are in a meeting. Use this page when you need the price for a target markup, the price ladder, a profit goal above break-even, and an export you can attach to a pricing memo. The formulas match. The dedicated page keeps the three questions side by side so changing cost updates the ladder.

No. Cost, price, and fixed costs are calculated on the device. You can download PDF, Excel, or CSV or send a text note to your phone. There is no account. The result is a pricing aid, not a financial statement or tax advice.
