Guide

How to use it

Enter your income and expenses per month, week or year. Totals, remaining money, percentages, a simple chart and a 50/30/20 comparison update as you type.

Steps

  1. Add your income sources and choose whether amounts are per month, week or year.
  2. Adjust the preset expense categories (rent, utilities, groceries and more), or add and remove your own.
  3. Read your total income, expenses, what is left or the shortfall, each category's share of income and how you compare with the 50/30/20 rule.

A monthly plan that matches cash

Budget take-home pay, not gross wages. The 50/30/20 checkpoint puts about half of take-home toward needs (housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, basic transport, minimum debt payments), about 30 percent toward wants, and about 20 percent toward savings and extra debt payments. The tool converts weekly or yearly amounts onto the same footing and shows each category as a percent of income.

Worked example: $4,000 take-home

Needs about $2,000, wants about $1,200, savings about $800. If a biweekly net is $1,690.85, the monthly average is that amount times 26 divided by 12, or $3,663.51. Budget the average, not two checks, or two months a year will look richer than they are. Rent of $1,800 already consumes most of the needs band. The rule does not say to skip rent. It says the rest of the plan has to be honest about what is left.

Irregular months and business cash

Freelance income belongs in the plan at a conservative month, with surplus moved to a tax reserve. That reserve is a need, not a want. Annual insurance should be entered as a yearly figure so one month is not a fake crisis. A shortfall is money you do not have. Covering it from savings is a transfer, not new income. Get the take-home figure from a stub or from the paycheck calculator. Keep business margin on the profit margin calculator so a good sales month is not mistaken for personal spending money.

Frequently asked questions

How do I apply 50/30/20 to a real take-home paycheck?

Use monthly take-home pay, not gross salary. On $4,000 a month, needs are about $2,000, wants about $1,200, and savings or extra debt payments about $800. Needs are housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, basic transport, and minimum debt payments. Wants are eating out, subscriptions, and travel. Savings include an emergency fund and retirement contributions beyond a payroll 401(k) that already left the paycheck. If rent alone is $1,800, the 50 percent needs band is already tight; the rule is a checkpoint, not a reason to skip rent. The calculator tags each category and shows how far you are from those shares.

Should the income box be gross, and how do weekly bills convert?

Type take-home pay. A biweekly net of $1,690.85 is not $3,381.70 every month. $1,690.85 times 26 divided by 12 is $3,663.51 as a monthly average. Enter that monthly average, or enter the check and tell the tool the amount is per paycheck period it offers (month, week, or year) so it annualizes consistently. The 2026 tax and paycheck calculators on this site estimate take-home if you do not have a stub yet. Do not budget the gross $80,000 and then also subtract tax inside the expenses or you will show a shortfall that is not real.

What does a shortfall mean for the next month?

If expenses exceed income, the result is a shortfall, not a remaining balance you can spend. Cut wants first, then renegotiate a need you control, such as a phone plan, before you cut a minimum debt payment. A one-month shortfall covered by savings should be labeled a transfer from savings, not new income, or next month's plan will assume the transfer repeats. The percent-of-income column shows which single category is doing the damage. A category over 100 percent of income is a data error or a bill that belongs in a different month.

How do I use this with irregular freelance income?

Budget a baseline month from your lowest reliable month, and put surplus months into a tax and slow-month reserve rather than into wants. Self-employed people should treat the tax reserve as a need. If the freelance rate calculator says you should set aside 25 percent, that transfer is not optional spending. Annual expenses such as insurance can be entered as a yearly amount so the month you pay them does not look like a crisis and the other months do not look richer than they are.

Is the budget saved on a server?

Only if you tick Remember my budget on this device. That copy lives in this browser's local storage and is never uploaded. Untick it to delete it. Another browser or phone will not see it. Export PDF, Excel, or CSV when you want a copy you control, or send a plain-text note to your phone. There is no account. Anyone who uses this browser profile can open the saved budget, so do not use Remember on a shared computer.

How often should I revise the plan?

Redo it when take-home pay changes, when a rent or insurance bill changes, and at least once a quarter. A budget that matched January will lie in December if annual gifts and insurance were never entered. The 50/30/20 comparison is a guide for consumer cash flow. A business with inventory should also track gross margin on the profit margin calculator and keep business and household money in separate columns so a good sales month does not look like personal spending money.
