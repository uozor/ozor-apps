Guide

How to use it

Choose a state and the rate to use (state plus average local, state only, or your own exact rate). Choose whether your prices are before tax or already include tax, then list the items. Totals update as you type.

Steps

  1. Pick your state and the rate to use. For an exact rate, choose "My exact rate" and enter the combined rate from your receipt or your state revenue department.
  2. Choose whether the prices you enter are before tax or already include tax.
  3. List the items with quantity and price, untick "Taxable" for any exempt item, and read the tax and total.

Frequently asked questions

Is the sales tax calculator free?

Yes. The sales tax calculator is 100% free with no limits, no watermark and no paid tier.

Do I need an account or sign-up?

No. There is no account, sign-up or email required. Open the page and start using it.

Can I export or download my results?

Yes. Use Save results in the Free forever box to download your inputs and results as PDF, Excel (.xlsx) or CSV, or tap Send to phone to share them as a plain-text note (share sheet, copy, .txt file or QR code). Files are created in your browser and include the date generated.

How do I calculate sales tax?

Multiply the taxable amount by the rate. At 8.25%, tax on $100.00 is $100.00 × 0.0825 = $8.25, so the total is $108.25.

How do I take sales tax out of a total (reverse sales tax)?

Divide the total by 1 plus the rate. For a $108.25 total at 8.25%: $108.25 ÷ 1.0825 = $100.00 before tax, so the tax was $8.25.

Which states have no sales tax?

Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no statewide sales tax. Alaska lets local governments charge one, averaging about 1.82%.

Are these the exact rates for my city?

No. The state rate is exact, but the local figure is a population-weighted average from the Tax Foundation (as of July 1, 2026). Local rates vary by city, county and district, so use "My exact rate" with the rate from your receipt or state revenue department when you need to be precise.

Where do the rates come from?

State and average local rates come from the Tax Foundation, "2026 Sales Tax Rates, Midyear Update" (rates as of July 1, 2026). Tax rules on what is taxable (food, clothing, services) also differ by state.

Are my files or text uploaded anywhere?

No. This tool runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing you enter or open is sent to our servers or stored by us.
