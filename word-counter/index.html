Guide

How to use it

Paste or type your text. Every number updates instantly, which is handy for essays, social posts, SEO meta descriptions and ad copy limits.

Steps

  1. Paste or type your text into the box.
  2. Read the live counts for words, characters, sentences and paragraphs.
  3. Check reading time and top keywords.

Counts that match the way editors talk

Words split on whitespace. Characters are reported with spaces and without. Reading time uses about 238 words per minute. Speaking time uses about 150. A 600-word description is about two and a half minutes to read and about four minutes to speak. The keyword list skips common function words. HTML pasted from a template counts the tags, so paste the visible text if you want a reading length.

Lengths businesses actually hit

Meta descriptions are often cut near 150 to 160 characters. Many ad headlines sit under 30 characters. A short email that gets finished is often under 200 words. A teaching article commonly runs 800 to 1,500 words, or about four to seven minutes at 238 words per minute. Resume bullets are healthier at one or two lines, roughly 15 to 30 words. Paste a bullet from the resume tool if you cannot tell whether it has swollen. None of these are scores. They are limits you would rather see here than in a rejected upload.

What the keyword list will not do

Frequency is not a ranking. A product name at 8 percent of the words may be stuffing. A name that never appears may be a vague page. Languages that do not put spaces between words will not match an English word count. The character count still means something. Exports save the counts locally. The text itself never needs an account, and it is not uploaded.

Frequently asked questions

How are words, characters, and time counted?

Words are split on whitespace, so a hyphenated word is one word and a number is a word. Characters are shown with spaces and without them. Sentences are split on end punctuation. Paragraphs are blocks separated by blank lines. Reading time uses about 238 words per minute. Speaking time uses about 150 words per minute. A 600-word product description is about 2.5 minutes to read and about 4 minutes to read aloud. Those rates are averages for adult English, not a promise about your reader. The top-keyword list skips very common function words so you can see what you actually repeat.

What lengths matter for real business writing?

A search snippet is often cut near 150 to 160 characters, so write the meta description in this box and watch the character count with spaces. Many ad headlines are tighter than 30 characters. A short email people will finish is often under 200 words. A blog post that teaches something real is commonly 800 to 1,500 words, which is a reading time of roughly 4 to 7 minutes at 238 words per minute. The counter does not judge quality. It stops you from discovering the limit after you have pasted into an ad platform.

What does keyword repetition tell you, and what does it not?

If one product name is 8 percent of the words, the paragraph may be stuffing rather than explaining. If the name never appears, the page may be vague. The list is a frequency count, not a ranking score and not a guarantee that a search engine will treat the phrase as a topic. Proper names and SKUs count. The same word with different capitalization is grouped in the usual way the counter normalizes. Use it to edit, then read the paragraph aloud.

How should I use it with the resume and policy tools?

Resume bullets should usually be one or two lines, roughly 15 to 30 words. Paste a bullet here if you cannot tell whether it is bloated. Policy drafts from the policy generator run long because they have to cover topics; the counter tells you the length so you can see whether a section became a wall of text. Neither tool sends the text to the other. Copy and paste is the handoff, and it stays on the device.

Are there limits on length?

Very large pastes are counted locally and can take a moment. The page is built to handle a long document without uploading it. If the tab becomes slow, split the text. There is no account and no stored document. Export PDF, Excel, or CSV if you want the counts and the keyword list in a file, or send a note to your phone. The export is the counts, not a backup of a novel, unless the text is included in that export's inputs.

Does it count HTML, code, or languages other than English?

Tags and code are counted as they appear. A page of HTML will show a high word count that includes tags, which is not the reading length of the visible article. Paste the visible text if that is what you care about. Languages without spaces between words will not match an English word count; the character count is still meaningful. Emoji and accents count as characters. The reading-time constant is still the English average.
