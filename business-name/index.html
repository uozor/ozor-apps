    Guide

    How to use it

    Enter one or two keywords that describe your business, pick a style, and generate fresh ideas. Click Generate again for more. Always check trademark and domain availability before choosing.

    Steps

    1. Enter one or two keywords that describe your business.
    2. Choose a style: modern, classic, playful or tech.
    3. Generate ideas and shortlist your favorites.

    Ideas are not clearances

    Each style draws from its own prefix and suffix list and mixes in the keyword you type. Another click draws again. The list is not a search of existing companies and not a legal opinion. Modern, classic, playful, and tech change the tone of the filler words. They do not check a registry.

    A practical shortlist

    Generate several batches. Keep five names you can still spell an hour later. Say each one on a phone call. Look at it in the logo maker at favicon size. A name that only works in a long script will fail as an icon. Then search the trademark database for your class of goods, your state or national business registry, the domain, and the social handles. Budget the hour before you order packaging. A name that is free as an LLC can still infringe a federal mark.

    Tone and risk

    A playful suffix can undersell a firm that needs to look established. A generic word plus Co is hard to find in search. Do not pick a name that implies a license you do not hold. Export the shortlist and mark which checks you finished. The file is a worksheet you made locally, not a registration. International sales need a check in each country where you will actually sell.

    Frequently asked questions

    How does the generator build a name, and why do results change?

    It combines the keyword you type with a style list. Modern, classic, playful, and tech each have their own prefix and suffix pools. Each click draws again, so the list is not a ranked search and it is not exhaustive. A keyword such as bloom bakery can surface compounds and style words around those tokens. The generator does not check whether the name is already a company, a domain, or a trademark. Two people in different cities can be shown the same idea. Treat every line as a starting point you still have to clear.

    What makes a name usable for a small business?

    Say it aloud, spell it to someone over the phone, and see whether the obvious .com or a sensible alternative is something you can register. Short names are easier to put on a sign and a favicon. A playful suffix can undersell a firm that wants to look established, and a classic suffix can make a software product sound like a law office. The style control is there so you can steer that tone. Avoid names that are only a generic word plus Co if you will be impossible to find, and avoid names that describe a regulated profession you are not licensed for.

    What do I check before I print cards?

    Search the USPTO trademark database or your country's equivalent for the exact name and for close spellings in your class of goods. Search your state or national business registry for the legal entity name. Search the domain and the major social handles. A name that is free as an LLC in one state can still infringe a federal trademark. This page does not perform those searches. Budget an hour for clearance before you buy packaging. If you will operate under a name that is not your personal name, file the assumed-name or entity paperwork your state requires.

    How should I shortlist?

    Generate several batches, copy the five you can still remember an hour later, and discard any you hesitate to spell. Check them in the logo maker at a small size. A name that only works in a long script will fail as an app icon. Ask someone outside the business to repeat the name back after hearing it once. Export the shortlist to PDF or a spreadsheet so you can mark which domains you checked. The export is a list you made, not a legal opinion.

    Can I use a name the tool suggested?

    Only after you clear it. The words in the style lists are ordinary English, and your keyword is yours to type, but the combination can still match an existing brand. We do not grant a license to a name, and we do not claim the ideas are unique. If you want a name that is also a registered domain, check the registrar before you fall in love with the spelling. International use needs a check in each country where you will sell.

    Are my keywords uploaded?

    No. Generation runs in the browser. There is no account and no server-side model. PDF, Excel, and CSV exports are local. The keyword is still visible on your screen and in any file you download.
