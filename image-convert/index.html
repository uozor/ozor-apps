    PNG, JPG, and WEBP

    JPG is the interchange format for marketplaces, email, and older tools. PNG keeps transparency and sharp edges and is the right file for a logo or a screenshot. WEBP is usually smaller than JPEG at the same quality and is what you want on a modern site. This page does not resize. Quality from 0.1 to 1 applies to JPG and WEBP. 0.9 is a light touch. PNG ignores it. JPG paints transparency with the background color you choose.

    A catalog batch

    Convert once from the master, not from a JPEG you already saved three times. Keep the SKU in the file name. Leave the transparent PNG of the logo alone and convert only the product photographs. If the destination is a web page at 1600 pixels, compress after the conversion. A 6000-pixel JPG is still a 6000-pixel JPG. HEIC from an iPhone opens only when the browser can decode it. Otherwise export JPEG from Photos first. Safari is the browser that usually can. A renamed PDF will fail decode, because the extension is not trusted.

    Color and privacy

    Drawing to a canvas can shift a wide-gamut photo into standard color. Check one frame at 100 percent before you convert a season of shots. Nothing is uploaded. A closed tab drops the batch, so download as you go. The compressor is the next step when the pixels are right but the file is heavy.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which format should a business actually pick?

    JPG for photographs you will upload to a marketplace, an email, or a tool that rejects newer formats. PNG when you need transparency or hard edges: logos, stamps, screenshots with text. WEBP when the image lives on a modern website and size matters. A logo exported only as a small JPEG will look soft on a retina screen and will show a box wherever the background is not white. Convert that asset to PNG or keep the SVG from the logo maker. Photographs converted to PNG get larger, not clearer.

    What happens to transparency, and what does quality 0.9 mean?

    JPG cannot store transparency. Transparent pixels are painted with the background color you pick, white by default. Set that color to your page color if the logo will sit on a tint, or do not convert that file to JPG. Quality runs from 0.1 to 1 for JPG and WEBP. 0.9 is a light touch and keeps more detail than the compressor's 75 percent default. 1 is still a lossy JPEG, not a mathematical copy. PNG ignores the quality number because it is lossless. Batch conversion uses the same format, background, and quality for every file you selected.

    Can I convert HEIC, and what fails?

    Only if the browser can decode the file. Safari often opens iPhone HEIC photos. Many desktop browsers cannot, and the page will say it could not read the image. On the phone you can set the camera to Most Compatible so new shots are JPEG, or export a JPEG from the Photos app and then convert. AVIF and some CMYK files fail the same way. Rename a .jpg that is actually a PDF and it will fail decode. The extension is not trusted; the browser has to draw the pixels.

    How do I convert a folder of product shots without mixing them up?

    Select the batch, pick JPG or WEBP, and download each result. Keep the original file name in your own folder so the SKU in the name survives. Do not convert the only copy of a transparent PNG to JPG and delete the PNG. Color shifts are possible when a wide-gamut photo is drawn to a standard canvas. For catalog work, compare one image at 100 percent zoom before you convert hundreds. There is no upload queue and no account, so a closed tab is a lost batch if you have not downloaded.

    What size should the converted file be?

    Conversion here does not resize. A 6000-pixel PNG becomes a 6000-pixel JPG, which may still be too large to email. Compress after you convert, or compress first if you already know the target width. A reasonable product image for the web is 1600 to 2000 pixels on the long side. A social avatar can be 512. Print shops often want the original pixels or a PDF, not a twice-converted JPEG. Each conversion that is lossy throws away a little more detail, so convert once from the master.

    Are the images uploaded?

    No. Decoding and encoding happen in the browser. Nothing is stored by GWB and there is no sign-up. You download each file locally. The same privacy boundary is why a failed file cannot be recovered from our side: we never had it.
