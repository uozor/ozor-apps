Guide

How to use it

Answer a few questions and get a customizable draft. These are templates, not legal advice; have a lawyer review for your jurisdiction.

Steps

  1. Enter your business name, website, contact email and location.
  2. Pick the policy type: privacy, terms or refund.
  3. Copy or download the draft and review it before publishing.

Drafts, not a law firm

The generator fills a plain-English privacy, terms, or refund draft with the business name, site, email, location, and the topics you tick. It is a starting point for a small site. It is not legal advice, not a GDPR record, not a PCI certificate, and not a HIPAA notice. Have counsel read it if you take payments, run ads, serve children, handle health data, or sell into the EU or UK. Delete any sentence that describes a business you do not run.

What a true privacy section contains

What you collect, why, who else sees it, how long you keep it, and how a person emails you. If a processor charges the card, say that you do not store the full number if that is true. If you show ads or analytics, say so. A 30-day refund you will not honor is worse than a shorter window you will. Custom work, digital goods, and food often need an exception written in the open. Match the refund text to what your payment platform requires and to what the counter will actually do.

Publishing

Put the text on a page you control and date it. Update it before you add a pixel or a newsletter, not after a complaint. This site's own practices are described on the GWB privacy policy and terms. Those pages are about GWB, not a template for your shop. Export a snapshot for your files. The generator does not host the policy and will not notice when your business changes. The draft is assembled locally.

Frequently asked questions

Are these policies legal, and what do I still have to do?

They are plain-English drafts for a small business, not legal advice and not a filing. Laws differ by country, state, and industry. A shop that sells to California residents, takes cards, and runs ads has different duties than a hobby page. Have a lawyer review the draft before you rely on it, especially if you handle health data, children's data, or customers in the EU or UK. The generator fills in the business name, site, email, and location you type and the topics you tick, such as cookies or payments. You must edit anything that does not describe how you actually work.

What should a privacy draft cover for a typical small site?

Say what you collect, why, who you share it with, how long you keep it, and how a person can reach you. If you use a card processor, name that role and say you do not store full card numbers if that is true. If you use analytics or ads, say so. If you sell only to businesses in one state and collect nothing but an email, do not claim a global compliance program you do not run. Accuracy matters more than length. A short true policy is better than a long copied one.

How should I think about terms and refunds?

Terms of service say what the customer may do, how purchases work, and that the law of a place you name will govern. A refund policy should match what your payment processor and your advertising platforms require, and it should match what you will actually do at the counter. A 30-day window you will not honor is worse than a 7-day window you will. Digital goods, custom work, and perishable food are often treated differently. Write the exception in the draft rather than hoping no one notices.

How do I publish and maintain the draft?

Copy the text into a page you control, or download the file and convert it to the format your site uses. Date it. When you add a new tool, a newsletter, or a pixel, update the policy before you turn the feature on. Keep the old date in your own notes so you know what changed. This generator does not host the policy and does not update it when your business changes. The export to PDF or Excel is a snapshot of the draft on the day you made it.

What is out of scope?

The drafts are not a GDPR record of processing, a PCI attestation, a HIPAA notice, an employee handbook, or a shareholder agreement. They do not choose your entity type. They do not register you for sales tax. If a sentence feels broader than your business, delete it. If a sentence feels missing, that is a question for counsel, not a reason to invent a legal rule in the text box.

Are my business details uploaded?

No. The draft is assembled in the browser. There is no account. PDF and Excel exports are local. You are still about to publish the text on your own site, and that published page is public on purpose.

Private by design: everything runs in your browser. Your files and text never leave your device.
