Guide

How to use it

Enter your pay, state and filing status. We apply the 2026 federal brackets and standard deduction, your state's 2026 brackets, and payroll taxes, then show your take-home pay.

Steps

  1. Enter your salary and choose whether it is annual, monthly or hourly.
  2. Pick your state and filing status, and add any pre-tax 401(k) contributions.
  3. Read your federal, state, Social Security and Medicare taxes and your take-home pay per year, month, two weeks and week.

2026 wage-tax reference

This estimate is for wages in tax year 2026. Federal ordinary brackets and the standard deduction follow IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32. Employee Social Security is 6.2 percent up to the Social Security Administration wage base of $184,500. Medicare is 1.45 percent of all wages, plus 0.9 percent Additional Medicare Tax above $200,000 single or head of household, $250,000 married filing jointly, and $125,000 married filing separately. The 401(k) elective deferral modeled here stops at $24,500. State tables follow the Tax Foundation's 2026 individual income-tax compilation: standard deduction, brackets, and a personal exemption or credit where that table records one. Sources: the IRS 2026 inflation adjustments, the SSA 2026 COLA fact sheet, and the Tax Foundation 2026 state brackets.

Worked example: $80,000, single, Texas

Taxable income is 80,000 minus the $16,100 standard deduction, or $63,900. Federal tax is 10 percent of $12,400 ($1,240), plus 12 percent of the next $38,000 ($4,560), plus 22 percent of the remaining $13,500 ($2,970), which totals $8,770. Social Security is $4,960. Medicare is $1,160. Total tax is $14,890 and take-home pay is $65,110. The marginal federal bracket is 22 percent. The effective rate on gross pay is 18.61 percent. A $5,000 pre-tax 401(k) saves 22 percent, or $1,100, of federal income tax and does not reduce FICA. Cash in hand becomes $61,210.

Same wages in Georgia

Georgia's simplified 2026 row is 5.19 percent after a $12,000 single standard deduction. State tax is (80,000 minus 12,000) times 0.0519, or $3,529.20. Federal and FICA do not change, so take-home pay falls to about $61,581. Nine states in this model have no wage income tax. Washington's capital-gains tax is not a wage tax, so wages show zero state income tax. City taxes and California SDI are not included.

What to do with the number

Match the lines to a pay stub before you change a W-4. Sole proprietors should not paste profit into the salary field: self-employment tax is 15.3 percent of 92.35 percent of net earnings and is a different calculation. Use the paycheck calculator for hourly and overtime, and the budget calculator once you know monthly take-home. Exports are labeled estimates, not a return.

Frequently asked questions

Which 2026 figures does this calculator use?

Federal brackets and standard deductions follow IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 for tax year 2026, including the inflation adjustments described in the IRS newsroom note on the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The standard deduction used here is $16,100 single or married filing separately, $32,200 married filing jointly, and $24,150 head of household. Social Security is 6.2 percent of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Medicare is 1.45 percent of all wages, plus the 0.9 percent Additional Medicare Tax on wages above $200,000 single or head of household, $250,000 married filing jointly, or $125,000 married filing separately. Employee 401(k) deferrals are capped in the model at the 2026 elective deferral limit of $24,500. State brackets, standard deductions, and personal exemptions or credits follow the Tax Foundation compilation of state individual income tax rates and brackets for 2026. We re-check those published tables when we update the tax year and do not invent rates.

Walk me through an $80,000 single filer in Texas.

Texas has no wage income tax in this model. Gross wages $80,000 minus the $16,100 standard deduction leaves $63,900 of federal taxable income. Tax is $1,240 on the first $12,400 at 10 percent, $4,560 on the next $38,000 at 12 percent, and $2,970 on the remaining $13,500 at 22 percent, which is $8,770 of federal income tax. Social Security is $80,000 times 0.062 = $4,960. Medicare is $80,000 times 0.0145 = $1,160. Combined tax is $14,890, so take-home pay is $65,110 before any benefit premiums. The marginal federal rate on the next dollar is 22 percent, but the effective rate on gross pay is 18.61 percent because of the standard deduction, the lower brackets, and payroll taxes. A pre-tax 401(k) of $5,000 stays inside the 22 percent bracket and lowers federal income tax by exactly $1,100, to $7,670. Payroll taxes do not fall, because 401(k) deferrals are still FICA wages. Cash in hand becomes $61,210, and $5,000 sits in the retirement account.

How is a state with a flat tax different, such as Georgia?

Georgia's 2026 table in this tool is a flat 5.19 percent after a standard deduction of $12,000 single or $24,000 married filing jointly, with no personal exemption in the simplified table. On $80,000 of single wages the state tax is (80,000 minus 12,000) times 0.0519 = $3,529.20. Federal income tax and FICA stay the same as the Texas example, so total tax is about $18,419 and take-home pay is about $61,581. States with graduated brackets, exemptions, or credits will not match a flat percentage of gross pay. The calculator applies the state's standard deduction and, where the Tax Foundation table records one, a personal exemption or credit. It does not apply city taxes, school-district taxes, or state disability insurance such as California SDI.

What does the calculator leave out on purpose?

It estimates wage income only. It does not apply the Child Tax Credit, Earned Income Credit, education credits, itemized deductions, the qualified business income deduction, capital gains, unemployment, or local wage taxes. Head of household and married filing separately use the published schedules in the engine; they are not a substitute for a return that has dependents or community-property rules. Self-employed people also owe self-employment tax of 15.3 percent on 92.35 percent of net earnings, half of which is deductible, and they usually make quarterly estimates. Use the freelance rate calculator for pricing and a tax professional or the IRS withholding estimator before you change a W-4. Washington taxes certain capital gains but not wages, so this tool shows zero state income tax on wages there.

How should a small business use the result in its books?

Compare the federal income tax, state income tax, Social Security, and Medicare lines with a recent pay stub before you rely on the annual figure. If you are an S corporation owner, wages and distributions are not the same number: only reasonable compensation runs through this wage calculator. Sole proprietors should not type profit into the salary box and treat the result as their full tax. Record employee withholdings as liabilities until you deposit them. When you export PDF, Excel, or CSV, the file is built in the browser and labeled as a 2026 estimate, not a filing. Pair the take-home figure with the monthly budget calculator so the household budget starts from cash you actually receive.

Are my numbers uploaded, and can I save them?

No upload. The brackets are a JavaScript table in your browser. Salary, state, filing status, and 401(k) amount stay on the device. Nothing is written to a GWB account because there is no account. Save results downloads a PDF, Excel workbook, or CSV that you generate locally, or Send to phone shares a plain-text note. Those files include the date and a reminder that the figures are estimates, not tax advice. Clearing the page or your browser storage removes the inputs.

Private by design: everything runs in your browser. Your files and text never leave your device.
