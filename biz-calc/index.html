Guide

How to use it

Type your numbers into any calculator. Results update instantly. Use any currency; amounts are shown with two decimals.

Steps

  1. Pick the calculator you need: margin, sales tax, loan or break-even.
  2. Type your numbers; results update instantly.
  3. Use the results to set prices or plan financing.

Four calculations, one page

Margin is profit over price. Markup is profit over cost. Added sales tax is amount times rate. Tax-inclusive price divided by 1 plus the rate returns the pre-tax amount. The loan payment uses the standard monthly amortization formula. Break-even units are monthly fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit. A simple ROI on a campaign is gain minus cost, divided by cost.

Defaults you can check by hand

Cost 40 and price 65: profit 25, margin 38.46 percent, markup 62.5 percent. Amount 100 at 8.875 percent: tax 8.88 and total 108.88. A $25,000 loan at 7.5 percent for 5 years: payment $500.95, interest about $5,056.92. Fixed costs $3,000 a month, price $25, variable cost $10: 200 units and $5,000 of revenue. A $2,000 ad buy that yields $2,600 of gross profit is a 30 percent ROI. Dividing by revenue instead of cost would flatter a weak spend.

When to open the dedicated tool

Use the profit margin calculator for a target price, a ladder, and a profit goal. Use the sales tax calculator for 2026 state rates and mixed baskets. Use the loan calculator for a schedule and extra principal. Fixed costs on this page are per month. A yearly rent figure typed here without dividing by 12 overstates the units you must sell.

Frequently asked questions

Margin, markup, and a quick price check.

On a cost of 40 and a price of 65, profit is 25, margin is 38.46 percent, and markup is 62.5 percent. Margin divides by price. Markup divides by cost. The dedicated profit margin page adds a target-price formula, a price ladder, and a profit goal. This box is the same two-line math for when you only need the three numbers. Zero cost makes markup undefined, and the box says so instead of showing a fake percent.

Sales tax on 100 at 8.875 percent, added or removed.

Added: tax is 8.88 and the total is 108.88, with cent rounding. Removed: if 108.88 already includes 8.875 percent tax, the pre-tax price is 108.88 divided by 1.08875, which lands back on 100, and the embedded tax is 8.88. Tick Amount already includes tax only for the second case. This box is one amount and one rate. For several items, exempt lines, and 2026 state average rates, use the sales tax calculator. Do not add tax on top of a price that already includes it.

A $25,000 loan at 7.5 percent for 5 years.

Monthly rate is 0.075 divided by 12. Over 60 months the payment is $500.95. Total paid is about $30,056.92 and total interest is about $5,056.92. The formula is the standard fully amortizing payment. There is no extra-payment field and no schedule on this page. When you need the month-by-month split or a down payment, open the loan calculator. Interest here is not a tax deduction by itself; business-interest limits and personal mortgage rules are separate and are not applied.

Break-even at $3,000 a month of fixed costs.

Fixed costs in this box are per month. Contribution per unit at a $25 price and a $10 variable cost is $15. $3,000 divided by 15 is 200 units exactly, and revenue at break-even is $5,000. If your fixed costs were typed as a yearly number, divide by 12 first or you will think you need 2,400 units a month. The profit margin page can take a profit goal above break-even. A price that is not above variable cost has no break-even.

A simple ROI beside these four boxes.

Return on a spend is gain minus cost, divided by cost. A $2,000 ad campaign that produces $2,600 of gross profit is ($2,600 minus $2,000) divided by $2,000 = 30 percent ROI. Do not divide by revenue or you will call a weak campaign a success. Margin tells you what each sale keeps. ROI tells you what a specific outlay returned. They answer different questions. This page does not discount future cash flows or compute an internal rate of return.

Are the amounts uploaded?

No. Every box runs in the browser. You can export PDF, Excel, or CSV or send a text note to your phone. There is no account. Results are arithmetic, not tax, legal, or lending advice.

Private by design: everything runs in your browser. Your files and text never leave your device.
